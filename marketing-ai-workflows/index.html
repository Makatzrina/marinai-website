AI WORKFLOWS & SMARTER EXECUTION

Less repetitive work.
More considered marketing.

An AI marketing workflow connects a specific task, appropriate information, a tool or model, and a human review process. The goal is not to automate everything. It is to make a useful part of the work more consistent and manageable.

Who this is for

For marketing teams whose time is absorbed by repeatable research, content preparation, manual handoffs, lead processing, or reporting.

Start with the work, not the automation

We map the current process: what starts it, which information it needs, where decisions happen, and what happens when something goes wrong. A workflow that is unclear when done manually usually needs simplifying before it needs automation.

Not every step needs AI. Some tasks are better handled by a template, a rule, or an existing integration. AI is more useful where the task involves interpretation or drafting and where its output can be reviewed against meaningful criteria.

Where a focused pilot may help

Possible applications depend on your business, available data, and existing systems. The following are examples to assess, not promises of autonomous performance.

  • Research summaries with source links and a verification step
  • Content briefs and draft variations that follow brand guidelines
  • Lead handoff preparation with human qualification and approval
  • Recurring report preparation, with source metrics checked before distribution
  • Reusable internal knowledge workflows with access and data boundaries

Human oversight is part of the design

A useful workflow specifies who approves output, how errors are caught, and which actions require explicit permission. Customer-facing claims, paid campaigns, publication, and sensitive information should not be left to an unreviewed output simply because a tool can execute the next step.

Before connecting systems, we consider what data is necessary, whether the selected tools are permitted to process it, who has access, and how failed runs are handled. We avoid putting confidential customer information into experiments without an agreed basis and safeguards.

Test, document, and hand over

The pilot should be assessed against representative tasks, including edge cases. We look at time saved alongside accuracy, quality, review effort, and recovery from failure. A process that looks fast in a demonstration may behave differently with real inputs.

Implementation support can include workflow specifications, a focused prototype, testing criteria, and guidance for the people using it. Integrations, technical delivery responsibilities, maintenance, and third-party costs are agreed as part of the project scope.

Questions about this service

Can AI publish or contact customers automatically?+

It may be technically possible, but that does not make it appropriate. We agree permissions, quality checks, brand risks, and compliance requirements first. Human review is the default for a new customer-facing pilot.

Will we need to change all our tools?+

Not necessarily. The first review looks at existing tools and integrations. A simpler process or a targeted improvement may be enough; a platform migration is not the default recommendation.
